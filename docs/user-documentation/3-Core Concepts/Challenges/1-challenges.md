# Challenges

Challenges are goal-based programmes that motivate customers to take specific actions over time in exchange for rewards. Instead of a one-time discount, a challenge keeps customers engaged across multiple visits or purchases - rewarding them as they progress and creating a sense of momentum and achievement along the way.

Think of a challenge as a game your customers play with your brand. You set the rules, define what they need to do to win, and decide what they get when they succeed. Challenges work especially well for building habits, increasing visit frequency, and making your loyalty programme feel exciting rather than transactional.

---

## The Two Types of Challenges

Qubriux supports two fundamentally different challenge structures. The right one depends on what behaviour you want to drive.

---

### Milestone-Based Challenges

A milestone-based challenge is a progressive challenge made up of one or more stages. Customers move through the milestones in sequence - each milestone has a condition they must meet, and when they meet it, they unlock a reward and advance to the next stage. Completing all milestones completes the challenge.

This is the right type when you want to reward a specific series of behaviours or build a narrative arc - "complete three things and win the prize."

**How milestones work:**
Each milestone defines what the customer must do to pass it. When a customer satisfies the condition for their current milestone, they immediately receive the reward attached to that milestone and automatically move on to the next one. Once they pass the final milestone, they complete the challenge and receive the final reward.

**Examples:**

> *A café chain creates a 3-milestone challenge called "Coffee Connoisseur." Milestone 1: buy any hot drink. Milestone 2: try a cold brew. Milestone 3: purchase a pastry. Customers who complete all three in the challenge window earn a free drink. Each milestone unlocks a small badge so customers can see their progress, keeping them motivated to finish.*

> *A fashion retailer runs a "New Season Explorer" challenge. Milestone 1: browse the new arrivals section. Milestone 2: make any purchase from new arrivals. Milestone 3: leave a review. Customers who complete the full journey earn double loyalty points on their next purchase. The milestones guide customers through a natural discovery journey the brand wants them to take.*

---

### Streak Challenges

A streak challenge rewards customers for doing the same thing consistently over and over again - daily, weekly, or monthly. There are no separate stages to pass through. Instead, the customer must keep their streak alive by completing the required action within each time period. Miss one period, and the streak resets.

This is the right type when you want to build a habit or reward consistent, repeated behaviour rather than a one-time journey.

**How streaks work:**
You define the action, the frequency (daily, weekly, or monthly), and how many times it must be completed in a row to win. Each time the customer completes the action within the required period, their streak count goes up. When their streak reaches the target, they complete the challenge and receive the reward.

**Examples:**

> *A fitness brand runs a "Weekly Warrior" streak challenge. Customers must make a purchase every week for 4 weeks in a row. Anyone who maintains a 4-week streak earns 1,000 loyalty points. The challenge runs monthly, so customers always have a new streak to chase.*

> *A restaurant chain launches a "Daily Lunch Club" streak challenge. Customers must order lunch 5 days in a row. Each day they complete an order, they see their streak grow in the app. On day 5, they unlock a free dessert. The daily frequency makes the challenge feel urgent and keeps customers coming back every single day.*

> *A grocery app wants to increase basket frequency. They run a "Monthly Regular" streak - customers who place at least one order per week for 4 consecutive weeks receive a $20 cashback reward. The challenge targets customers who currently shop once a month and nudges them toward a weekly habit.*

## Rewards

When a customer completes a milestone or finishes a streak, they earn a reward. You choose what that reward is when you set up the challenge. Challenges support three reward types, and you can combine multiple rewards at the same milestone or completion point.

---

### Loyalty Points

The customer receives a fixed number of loyalty points, credited directly to their wallet. This is the most common challenge reward - it ties challenge participation directly into your loyalty programme, giving customers more to spend while reinforcing the habit of engaging with your brand.

**Example:**
> *Completing milestone 1 awards 100 points, milestone 2 awards 200 points, and completing the full challenge awards a bonus 500 points. Customers can see the total points on offer before they even start, which gives them a compelling reason to attempt the challenge.*

---

### Badge

The customer receives a digital badge that is displayed on their profile or in your app. Badges are recognition rewards - they signal status and achievement, which is particularly powerful for customers who are competitive or community-driven. Badges also serve as a visible reminder of what a customer has accomplished.

**Example:**
> *A brand creates a "Summer Sprint" challenge. Every customer who completes it earns an exclusive "Summer Champion" badge on their profile. The badge is only available for the duration of the summer campaign, making it feel rare and worth pursuing. Customers who earn it are proud to show it off, and those who haven't feel motivated to earn theirs before the challenge ends.*

---

### Offer

The customer receives an offer from your Offers library - a discount, free product, or any other reward you have created. Using an offer as a challenge reward gives you full flexibility: you can reward customers with a personalised discount code, free shipping, or even a Buy X Get Y deal the moment they complete the challenge.

**Example:**
> *A skincare brand runs a "Skin Routine Builder" milestone challenge. Customers who complete all three milestones unlock a **30% off their next purchase** offer, delivered automatically to their email the moment the final milestone is passed. The discount is the exclusive prize for finishing, giving customers a strong financial reason to see the challenge through.*

---

## How Customers Join a Challenge

You control whether customers are automatically entered into a challenge or whether they need to opt in themselves. This distinction matters a lot for how customers experience the challenge.

---

### Auto-Enrolment

When auto-enrolment is on, every customer in your target audience is entered into the challenge automatically when it goes live. They don't need to do anything - they're already participating the moment the challenge starts. Their progress begins tracking from the first qualifying action they take.

**When to use it:**
Auto-enrolment works best for broad challenges where you want maximum participation and don't want to rely on customers discovering and actively joining the challenge themselves. It's lower-friction but means customers may not feel they have chosen to participate.

**Example:**
> *A brand launches a "Welcome Week" challenge for all new customers automatically enrolled within their first 7 days. Customers simply buy once and see themselves already one milestone in - no sign-up required.*

---

### Opt-In Required

When opt-in is required, the challenge appears to eligible customers but they must actively choose to join before their progress is tracked. Only actions taken after a customer has joined count toward their milestones or streak.

**When to use it:**
Opt-in works best when you want customers who feel invested and committed. Customers who actively choose to join are more likely to see the challenge through, because they've made a conscious decision to participate. It also makes the challenge feel more like an exclusive programme customers are choosing to be part of.

**Example:**
> *A coffee brand launches a "Barista's Favourite" challenge visible to all customers in the app. Customers tap "Join Challenge" to start. Because they've opted in, they feel a stronger sense of commitment to follow through, leading to higher completion rates compared to an equivalent auto-enrolled challenge.*

---

## Participation Limits and Rejoining

By default a customer can only complete a challenge once. But you can configure challenges to allow customers to repeat them, which is particularly useful for streak challenges or ongoing monthly programmes.

**Participation limit**
Set a cap on how many total times any single customer can participate in the challenge across its entire run. This prevents customers from gaming a well-rewarded challenge by completing it over and over.

**Rejoin after completion**
Set a waiting period (in days) before a customer who has already completed the challenge can rejoin and attempt it again. For example, if you set a 30-day rejoin window, a customer who finishes the challenge on the 1st of the month can rejoin on the 1st of the following month.

**Example:**
> *A weekly streak challenge is designed to run permanently as an ongoing programme. The challenge allows unlimited participation with a 7-day rejoin window. Customers who complete the 4-week streak earn their reward, wait one week, then can start a fresh streak - creating a continuous engagement loop that keeps running month after month.*

## Choosing Your Audience

Just like offers, challenges can be targeted to a specific group of customers or opened to everyone.

**Include customers by:**
- **All customers** - your entire registered customer base
- **All leads** - contacts who haven't made a purchase yet
- **Segments** - groups of customers who share a behaviour or attribute
- **Tiers** - customers in a specific loyalty tier, such as Gold, Silver, or Bronze
- **Customer lists** - a manually selected group of specific customers

**Example:**
> *A brand runs an exclusive challenge only for their Gold tier members, rewarding their best customers with a premium badge and 2,000 points. Because the challenge is invisible to Silver and Bronze tier customers, it feels like a VIP perk, reinforcing the value of reaching Gold status.*

## Challenge Status

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Draft** | The challenge has been created but not published - still being configured |
| **Scheduled** | The challenge is set to go live at a future start date |
| **Active** | The challenge is live and customers are participating |
| **Ended** | The challenge end date has passed; no new progress or completions are possible |
| **Deactivated** | The challenge has been manually stopped before its end date |

---

## Customer Progress Status

Each customer participating in a challenge has their own status:

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Not Started** | The customer has been enrolled or opted in but hasn't taken any qualifying action yet |
| **Active** | The customer is currently in progress |
| **Completed** | The customer has finished all milestones or reached their streak target and earned the final reward |
| **Ended** | The challenge ended before the customer could complete it |
| **Usage Completed** | The customer has reached their maximum participation limit for this challenge |

## Putting It All Together - Real Scenarios

### Drive Weekly Visit Frequency

A casual dining chain wants to turn occasional visitors into weekly regulars.

- **Type:** Streak Challenge
- **Action:** Place any dine-in or takeaway order
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Target:** 4 consecutive weeks
- **Reward:** $25 cashback + "Regular" badge
- **Audience:** Customers who have visited 1–3 times in the past 90 days
- **Participation:** Auto-enrolment, rejoin after 7 days

Customers who are already occasionally visiting see the streak growing each week and feel motivated to not break it. The badge and cashback combination rewards both loyalty and status. Because the challenge auto-resets weekly, participants are continuously engaged.

---

### Introduce Customers to a New Product Range

An online retailer is launching a new product line and wants customers to discover and buy from it.

- **Type:** Milestone-Based Challenge
- **Milestone 1:** View any product from the new range → Award: 50 points
- **Milestone 2:** Add any product from the new range to cart → Award: 50 points
- **Milestone 3:** Complete a purchase from the new range → Award: 200 points + 15% off next order offer
- **Audience:** All existing customers
- **Participation:** Opt-in (customers choose to join via the app or email)
- **Duration:** 30 days

Each milestone rewards the customer for taking a step deeper into the new range, culminating in a purchase. The opt-in ensures only genuinely interested customers participate, leading to higher completion rates. The final 15% off offer gives customers a strong reason to make that first purchase.

---

### Reward Your VIP Customers Exclusively

A luxury brand wants to make their top-tier customers feel uniquely valued.

- **Type:** Milestone-Based Challenge
- **Milestone 1:** Spend $200 in a single transaction → Award: "Connoisseur" badge
- **Milestone 2:** Spend $200 in a second separate transaction → Award: 1,000 points
- **Milestone 3:** Spend $200 in a third transaction → Award: Exclusive "Elite" badge + VIP offer (free shipping for 3 months)
- **Audience:** Gold tier members only
- **Participation:** Auto-enrolment
- **Duration:** 90 days

Gold tier customers see an exclusive challenge designed specifically for them. The progressive badges give them status milestones to show off, while the final Elite badge and VIP offer feel genuinely premium. The 90-day window is long enough to feel achievable for high-value customers who already spend regularly.
