# Badges

Badges are digital awards you give to customers for doing something meaningful with your brand - completing a challenge, reaching a milestone, making their first purchase, or achieving a status worth recognising. They live permanently on a customer's profile, building up a visible record of what they've accomplished.

Beyond recognition, badges can carry real value. When a badge is awarded, it can automatically deliver loyalty points, an offer, or cashback directly to the customer - making the moment of earning a badge feel both meaningful and rewarding.

Badges also work as building blocks. You can use badge-earning as a trigger inside challenges, so earning one badge unlocks the next step in a progression. This lets you build sophisticated engagement programmes where customers move through a journey, earning recognition and rewards at each stage.

---

## The Two Types of Badges

---

### One-Time Badge

A one-time badge is awarded as recognition - it marks that a customer has achieved something specific. Once earned, it stays on their profile permanently as a signal of their status or accomplishment.

One-time badges are the most common type. They're ideal for marking firsts, milestones, and loyalty achievements: the first purchase, a spending milestone, completing a challenge, or reaching a certain tier.

**Examples:**

> *A coffee chain creates a "First Sip" badge awarded the first time any customer makes a purchase. It's the simplest form of recognition - a welcome marker that says this customer is part of the community. Customers who have it on their profile are more likely to feel connected to the brand.*

> *A fashion retailer creates a "Big Spender" badge for any customer who spends over $1,000 in a single transaction. The badge is rarely earned, which makes it feel genuinely exclusive. Customers who have it know their status is visible - and others aspire to it.*

> *A grocery chain creates a "5-Year Member" badge that is awarded to customers who have been active for five consecutive years. Long-term customers receive the badge automatically, reinforcing how valued their loyalty is.*

---

### Boost Effect Badge

A boost effect badge goes beyond recognition - it actively enhances how the customer experiences your programme while they hold it. Rather than simply marking an achievement, a boost badge applies a multiplier or benefit on top of your standard rules.

Boost effect badges are ideal for creating temporary elevated status, rewarding subscribers, or giving VIP customers a tangible advantage that others can see and aspire to.

**Examples:**

> *A loyalty programme creates a "Gold Boost" badge that doubles the points a customer earns on every purchase while they hold it. The badge is awarded to customers who subscribe to the premium tier. The moment they subscribe, their earn rate immediately improves - giving them a clear, compelling reason to subscribe and stay subscribed.*

> *A restaurant chain runs a "Weekend Warrior" badge during a seasonal campaign. Any customer who completes a streak challenge earns the badge, which applies a 1.5x points multiplier on all weekend purchases for the following month. The badge is time-limited and the boost expires at the end of the campaign - creating urgency to earn it before the window closes.*

> *A fashion brand gives a "New Season Explorer" badge to any customer who purchases from the new arrivals range within the first two weeks of launch. The badge applies a 2x points boost on all new arrivals purchases for 30 days, rewarding early adopters and driving momentum at the most important moment in the product launch.*

## Badge Rewards

When a badge is awarded to a customer, it can automatically deliver a reward at the same moment. You configure the reward when you create the badge - and it fires every time the badge is earned. You can attach multiple rewards to a single badge.

---

### Loyalty Points

The customer receives a fixed number of loyalty points, credited directly to their wallet the moment the badge is awarded. This connects badge achievement directly into your points programme, so earning a badge and earning points feel like one seamless moment.

**Example:**

> *A brand creates a "Challenge Champion" badge awarded to customers who complete any monthly challenge. The badge delivers 500 loyalty points instantly. Customers who finish the challenge see their points balance jump immediately - reinforcing that the effort was worth it and giving them something to spend on their next visit.*

---

### Offer

The customer receives an offer from your Offers library, delivered automatically when the badge is awarded. This lets you reward badge earners with a concrete discount, free product, or any offer you've created.

**Example:**

> *A skincare brand creates a "Skincare Believer" badge for customers who purchase from three different product categories. When the badge is awarded, it automatically delivers a **25% off your next order** offer. The offer is the payoff for exploring the range - and it arrives the moment the customer earns it, when the feeling of achievement is strongest.*

---

### Cashback

The customer receives a cashback amount credited directly to their wallet when the badge is awarded. The cashback sits in their balance, ready to spend on their next purchase.

**Example:**

> *A restaurant chain creates a "Loyal Regular" badge awarded after a customer's 10th visit in a calendar year. When the badge is earned, the customer automatically receives $10 cashback in their wallet - a thank-you for their consistency. The cashback is immediately visible in the app, giving them a reason to come back and use it.*

## Setting Up Your Badge

When you create a badge, you configure both what it looks like and how it behaves.

**Name and description**
The name customers see when the badge appears on their profile. The description explains what the badge means and how it was earned.

**Badge image and banner image**
The visual assets that represent the badge - the icon displayed on a customer's profile, and the banner shown when a customer earns or views the badge. These are what make a badge feel distinctive and worth collecting.

**Alias name, alias description, and alias banner**
If you operate in multiple markets or languages, you can set an alternative name, description, and banner image for different regions. The right version is shown automatically based on the customer's context.

**Deep link URL**
An optional link that takes the customer to a specific destination inside your app when they tap the badge - for example, the relevant offer, the challenge they completed, or a product page.

**Rewards**
The reward or rewards delivered to the customer when the badge is awarded. You can attach loyalty points, an offer, and/or cashback - in any combination.

## Controlling Who Can Earn a Badge

---

### Per-Customer Assignment Limit

You can set a cap on how many times a single customer can receive the same badge. By default, if you award the same badge multiple times through different journeys or challenges, a customer can accumulate it more than once. Setting a limit prevents this.

**Example:**

> *A challenge badge is awarded at the end of a monthly streak challenge. The challenge is designed to repeat every month. Setting a per-customer limit of **1** means no matter how many months a customer completes the streak, they only earn the badge once - keeping it as a permanent recognition marker rather than accumulating duplicates.*

> *A brand runs a special "Summer Champion" badge with a per-customer limit of **3**. Customers can earn the badge up to three times across the summer campaign - each time they earn it, they receive the attached 200-point reward. After the third time, they've reached their limit and can't earn it again.*

---

### Global Assignment Limit (Budget)

You can set a total cap on how many times a badge can be awarded across all customers combined. Once this limit is reached, no further customers can earn the badge - making it genuinely limited in availability.

**When to use it:**
Global limits work well for exclusive or rare badges where scarcity is part of the appeal - "only the first 500 customers to earn this" - or for controlling the total cost of the attached reward.

**Example:**

> *A brand creates a "Founding Member" badge with a global limit of 1,000. Only the first 1,000 customers who complete the onboarding journey receive it. Once 1,000 badges have been awarded, the badge is no longer available - making it a permanent mark of early membership that feels rare and meaningful.*

---

### Segment Targeting

You can restrict which customers are eligible to receive a badge by attaching a segment to it. Only customers who fall within that segment can be awarded the badge - even if the badge is referenced in a journey or challenge that reaches a broader audience.

**Example:**

> *A retailer creates a "Loyal Local" badge restricted to customers in a specific city. Even if the badge is offered through a national challenge, only customers in that city are eligible to receive it - making it feel relevant and regional.*

---

## How Customers Earn Badges

Badges are awarded through the other modules in Qubriux. You create a badge once and then use it across as many programmes as you like.

**Through Challenges**
A badge can be a reward at any milestone in a challenge, or when a customer completes a streak. Customers see the badge as an incentive before they start, and receive it the moment they pass the milestone.

**Through Journeys**
A badge can be delivered as an action in any journey. When a customer reaches the step that awards the badge, it is added to their profile automatically.

**Through Loyalty Rules**
A badge can be part of a loyalty earn rule - awarded when a customer reaches a milestone spend, a points threshold, or as a tier benefit.

**Through Offers**
A badge can be delivered as part of an offer, allowing you to award recognition alongside a promotional reward.

**Earning a badge as a milestone condition**
A badge earned in one challenge can trigger a milestone in another. This lets you build badge chains - where completing a beginner challenge awards a badge that then unlocks the next challenge, creating a natural progression through your programme.

## Badge Status

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Active** | The badge is live and can be awarded to customers |
| **Inactive** | The badge has been deactivated and will not be awarded |

## Putting It All Together - Real Scenarios

### A Recognition Programme for a Coffee Chain

A café chain wants to build a collection of badges that recognise meaningful moments in a customer's journey - creating a profile that feels personal and shows how long they've been part of the brand.

**Badges created:**
- "First Sip" - awarded on a customer's first purchase → **100 welcome points**
- "Regular" - awarded after 10 total visits → **200 points**
- "Café Connoisseur" - awarded after trying 5 different drink categories → **$5 cashback**
- "Loyal for a Year" - awarded after 12 months of activity → **500 points + 15% off offer**
- "Big Spender" - awarded after a single order over $50 → **300 points**

**How they're awarded:** Each badge is a node inside an automated journey that fires on the qualifying event.

**Result:** Customers build up a profile of badges that tells the story of their loyalty. Each badge they earn is both a recognition and a reward, giving them a reason to come back and collect more. Customers who have been with the brand for years have a visible history that makes them feel genuinely valued - and proud to show off.

---

### A Badge-Driven Progression Challenge

An online retailer wants to guide new customers through their catalogue and reward them for exploring different product ranges. They design a milestone-based challenge where each stage awards a badge that also carries a reward.

**Challenge structure:**
- **Milestone 1:** Purchase from the Clothing category → Award: "Style Explorer" badge + 100 points
- **Milestone 2:** Purchase from the Footwear category → Award: "Sole Seeker" badge + 150 points
- **Milestone 3:** Purchase from the Accessories category → Award: "Full Look" badge + 200 points + 20% off next order offer

**Audience:** All new customers in the first 30 days

**Participation:** Auto-enrolled on signup

**Result:** New customers are immediately guided into a structured discovery journey. Each badge they earn is a visible milestone showing how far through the challenge they've come - and each carries a point reward that grows their balance. Customers who complete all three earn the full look badge and a strong reason to come back and spend their discount. The badges persist on their profile long after the challenge ends, reminding them of what they've explored.

---

### A Boost Badge for Premium Subscribers

A grocery delivery app wants to make subscribing to their premium tier feel immediately and tangibly different - not just cosmetically, but in how much the customer earns on every order.

**Badge created:** "Premium Boost" badge (Boost Effect type)
- **Boost applied:** 1.5x points multiplier on all purchases
- **Award:** Delivered automatically when a customer activates a premium subscription
- **Removed:** When the subscription lapses or is cancelled

**Attached reward:** 500 welcome points credited the moment the badge is awarded

**Result:** The instant a customer upgrades to premium, they see the badge appear on their profile and 500 points land in their wallet. Every order after that earns at the boosted rate - they can see the difference immediately. When the app prompts free users to upgrade, the badge and its boost effect are highlighted as the key benefit. Subscribers who consider cancelling are reminded exactly what they'd lose the moment the badge disappears.
